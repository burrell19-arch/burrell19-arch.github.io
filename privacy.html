Privacy Policy

How the Naruto Playlist Organizer handles Google and YouTube data.

Who uses the organizer and why

This personal tool uses YouTube API Services to organize approved playlists on the Lillith Archives channel. The owner supplies the roster and ordered video lists. A read-only Google authorization lets the organizer check channel identity, playlist IDs, titles, visibility, membership and order, and video availability. A separate authorization lets it create approved playlists and add approved videos. The organizer checks the channel and playlist ownership, uses the observations to prevent duplicate writes, and saves progress so an interrupted run can resume.

Where information goes

Google handles OAuth sign-in and YouTube requests. The organizer never receives the Google password. Credentials are restricted to the operator’s local profile and private GitHub Actions secrets. The scheduled workflow runs on GitHub. A private Google Cloud Storage checkpoint holds progress, pending operations and quota or error state. Private GitHub Actions reports are configured to expire after 14 days. Older API records can also be recoverable from private Git history, local files and Microsoft OneDrive versions or its recycle bin.

The operator does not sell Google user data or use it for advertising, profiling or model training. The public information site sets no application cookies, embeds no YouTube player and uses no analytics scripts. GitHub Pages may log requests for security. Google and Microsoft handle sign-in and storage on their own services under their respective policies. See Google’s Privacy Policy, GitHub’s Privacy Statement and Microsoft’s Privacy Statement.

How long records are kept

The owner’s original instructions are kept separately from observations returned by YouTube. Applicable API observations must be refreshed or deleted within 30 calendar days. During active processing the organizer rechecks playlist ownership and rotates checks of previously completed playlists against YouTube. The operator handles inactive records and recoverable historical copies separately. An updated checkpoint does not refresh an older report.

Request deletion or revoke access

Use the support contact on the organizer’s Google consent screen to request deletion or to ask the operator to revoke access. The operator’s procedure calls for stopping collection of the affected data, checking controlled copies and removing the applicable records as soon as possible, within seven calendar days of a direct deletion request or revocation through the organizer. For revocation through the organizer, its revocation command sends the affected token to Google immediately.

You can also remove access yourself in Google Account permissions. For revocation there, the deadline to delete the associated records is 30 calendar days, with removal as soon as possible. The operator checks for recoverable copies and records any outstanding exceptions. The operator cannot independently verify erasure of inaccessible provider backups. Deleting organizer records leaves playlists and videos on YouTube. Use YouTube to manage or delete them there.

Use of the organizer is also subject to the YouTube Terms of Service. The operator will inform the authorized user before a material change in the use of Google user data.
